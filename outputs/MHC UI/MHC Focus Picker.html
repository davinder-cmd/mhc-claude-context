V1 · Pillar grid RECOMMENDED

A categorized 2-column grid grouped by pillar. The user can scan the whole option space in one screen, with pillar dots in your secondary palette (spring green, aqua, mango, zesty green, lava orange) acting as a legend.

Why pick it

  • Browsing-first. Users see the breadth of what MHC supports — feels like a real product, not a survey.
  • Pillar groupings reinforce your taxonomy from the very first interaction.
  • Tiles are large enough for an icon + name; small enough to fit ~12 above the fold.

Watch out

  • One-pillar dominance. With 4 physical sub-categories, "physical" looks heavier than "social." Consider equalizing to 2–3 per group.
  • Single-select model isn't obvious from a grid layout. The selected-tile blue fill + check pip carry that load — keep both.

V2 · Tabbed list ALT

A single-column list with pillar tabs at the top. Each row carries a "why this matters" subtitle, so users get a hint of what they're signing up for.

Why pick it

  • Reads like a familiar mobile filter pattern (App Store, Spotify).
  • Subtitle copy makes ambiguous focuses ("Move every day") feel concrete.
  • Single-select feels natural in a vertical list with radio dots.

Watch out

  • Hides the breadth — only ~5 options visible at once, so users may pick the first plausible one and miss better fits.
  • "All" tab only useful if you can rank-order it well (ML/heuristics by user signals).

V3 · Hero pick BOLD

One recommended focus rendered large with reasoning, and a chip pool of alternates beneath. Treats the recommendation as the default and dismissal as the deviation.

Why pick it

  • Lowest-friction path — most users take the recommendation, especially if you've already asked qualifying questions earlier in onboarding.
  • The dark "Why this?" card is a brand moment — your first chance to look like a real coach, not a form.

Watch out

  • Only works if you have a real recommendation engine. Without it, this layout overpromises.
  • Chips compress the alternates — fine for fast scanners, but loses the explanatory subtitle that V2 carries.
