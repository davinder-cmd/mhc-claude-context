V1 · Step number rail

The rail shows session N of total. It's the same denominator the user sees on the program detail page, so it's recognizable, motivating, and uses data you already have.

Why pick it

  • Concrete progress without a time commitment we can't promise.
  • "Lesson 9 of 28" reads like a bookmark — exactly the metaphor "pick up where you left off" implies.
  • Big number on the rail keeps the visual rhythm of the time-of-day version you liked.

Watch out

  • Programs without sessions (open-ended habits) need a different rail. See V3.

V3 · Status word rail

Rail is a verb the user can act on: Continue, Resume, Start week 4, Wrap up. Computed from "days since last interaction" + "next session type."

Why pick it

  • Doesn't fake precision. If you only know "user hasn't opened this in 12 days," the rail says Resume, not "Tonight · 12 min."
  • Encodes urgency (Resume = orange, Continue = blue) without inventing time data.
  • Works for open-ended programs with no session count.

Watch out

  • The rail action words must stay short (≤9 chars) or they wrap awkwardly.
  • You'll need server logic to pick the right verb — easy, but not free.

V4 · Provider monogram rail

Replaces images with a typographic monogram of the provider name (BH, BA, SO) and a one-word program category.

Why pick it

  • Same visual rhythm as a logo, but it's just text — no asset pipeline.
  • Doubles as visual diversity (each rail is different) without being noisy.

Watch out

  • Provider monograms can collide (BH = Big Health and Beam Health). Falls down at scale.
  • Doesn't encode progress at all — that has to live in the body.

V5 · Week pill rail

D1 · Inline X button (form submit) Recommended

A small × in the top-right corner of each card. It's wrapped in a <form method="post" action="/programs/:id/dismiss"> — clicking it submits and the page re-renders without that program. Pure HTML, no JS.

Why pick it

  • Universally understood pattern — it's the same affordance as iOS notifications, email, etc.
  • Works on touch (small target — make it ≥32×32 in production) and desktop with no special handling.
  • Server is in full control: dismissed = soft-hide for 30 days, then re-surface, or permanently — your call.

Watch out

  • One-tap-to-dismiss is destructive without confirmation. Pair with a "Show again" link in the empty state, or with D3's menu for an "Undo last dismiss" pattern.
  • Forms inside a card with a stretched <a> link is fine because the form button has higher z-index and stops the link from receiving the click.

D2 · Persistent action row

"Hide" and "Remind me later" sit alongside the primary CTA as small chip-buttons. Always visible, always reachable.

Why pick it

  • No hidden affordances — discoverable on first scan.
  • Distinguishes "I'm done" (Hide) from "not now" (Remind me later) — a more humane choice than a single dismiss.

Watch out

  • Adds vertical density. With 3 cards stacked, the page can feel button-heavy.
  • Visually competes with the primary CTA. Keep "Hide" tertiary.

D3 · ⋯ menu via native <details>

<details> + <summary> gives you a click-to-open menu with zero JavaScript. Each menu item is a tiny <form> POST.

Why pick it

  • Holds multiple dismiss/snooze options without crowding the card.
  • Matches the "⋯" pattern users know from every native app.
  • Keyboard accessible by default; summary is focusable.

Watch out

  • Doesn't auto-close when you click outside (no JS!) — second click on the summary closes it. Acceptable for this volume of options.
  • Mobile tap target is the small ⋯; pad summary to ≥32×32.

D4 · "Not now" snooze (no destructive option)

Drop "Hide forever" entirely — every dismiss is a 7-day snooze. After the snooze window, the program re-surfaces.

Why pick it

  • No regret. Users can never accidentally lose access to a program they paid for.
  • Maps to real behavior: most "I don't want to see this" decisions are situational, not final.
  • Requires no confirmation modal.

Watch out

  • Power users who quit a program want it gone, not snoozed. Provide a deeper "Manage programs" page (see D5) for them.

D5 · Footer "Manage" link

No per-card dismiss at all. A small footer below the rail links to a dedicated Manage programs page where users can snooze, hide, or quit each program with full context.

Why pick it

  • Cleanest cards. Zero affordance noise.
  • Forces a deliberate decision — fewer accidental hides, fewer "where did my program go?" support tickets.
  • Easiest to ship; no per-card form endpoints.

Watch out

  • High friction. Users who want one program out of their face have to navigate to another page.
  • Manage page becomes a required design — not a small undertaking.

Recommendation

Ship V1 (Step number rail) + D1 (inline × dismiss). If you want to give users a softer landing, pair D1 with D3's <details> menu so the × is one of several options ("Remind me later", "Hide from home").

Falls back gracefully: programs without a session count fall back to the V5 "Week N of M" rail. Programs without weeks (open-ended habits) fall back to V3 "Continue / Resume." One template, three rail variants, server picks.

Server contract: the dismiss form POSTs to /programs/:id/dismiss and returns to the home page. Persist as dismissedAt, re-surface after 30 days unless user explicitly chose "Hide forever" from the ⋯ menu.
