Yes — same overall pattern as peer invites: a dedicated invites block at the top of home with accept/decline. But three things differ enough that it's worth being deliberate: who's inviting, how late they're joining, and how the invite re-uses the active-challenge slim banner.

Recommendation · same block, with two refinements

Use one unified Pending invites block at the top of home for both invite types. Don't split into "Team invites" + "Peer invites" — the user just sees "things asking for my decision." Sort by urgency (closing soonest first), cap at 3 visible with "See all →" overflow.

Refinement 1 · Visual differentiation in the avatar

Team invites use a square/rounded-rect avatar with the team mark; peer invites use a circular avatar with personal initials. Same row layout, different shape signals different scope. No badges or labels needed — the shape carries the meaning.

Refinement 2 · Late-join transparency

When a team invite arrives during the active stage (challenge already running), the detail line surfaces two things peer invites never need:

  • Days elapsed / remaining — "5 days in, 16 days remaining" — so the user knows what they're walking into.
  • Current team position — "Aurora is currently #4 of 38" — context for whether joining feels rewarding or doomed.

Peer invites don't need this because the challenge starts on accept; team challenges start independently of the invitee.

Edge case · Invite + active-challenge slim banner

If the user is already in a team challenge and gets invited to another active team challenge (rare but possible — multi-team employer rollouts), the slim "you can still join" banner from the original challenge wins. The new invite still shows in the Pending invites block. We don't double-stack banners.

Edge case · Auto-invite from team page browsing

If joining is open (no captain approval needed), browsing the team page and selecting "Join Aurora" should bypass the invite flow entirely — that's a direct join, not an invitation. The Pending invites block is reserved for invites sent to the user.

What changes vs. peer · summary

  • Block: same — Pending invites, top of home, above hero, max 3.
  • Avatar shape: different — square for team, circle for peer.
  • Inviter copy: different — "Team {name} invited you" vs. "{Person} challenged you".
  • Detail line: different — surfaces elapsed/remaining + team rank for active-stage team invites.
  • Decline copy: different — "Pass" feels right for team (you're saying no to a group); "Decline" for peer (you're saying no to a person). Subtle but the social weight is different.
  • Server contract: extend invitePending to cover both sources; client treats them uniformly except for the avatar mark and detail-line builder.
