Why this palette fights you

Four structural problems, all measurable. None of them are taste.

10

Steps of drift in the AA line

Text hits 4.5:1 at Coffee 600 but not until Yellow 1600. The step number carries no consistent meaning across families — so "use 800 for primary" is unbuildable.

⅓

of the range in the dark half

Steps 100→800 cover 44–66 points of lightness. Steps 800→1600 cover only 16–32. Nine steps, barely any change — which is why reaching for a darker shade never gives you one.

2

Families that reverse

Grey and Ocean each contain a step perceptually lighter than the one before it. Ocean 1000 is also a byte-identical duplicate of Ocean 800. (Corrected 5 Aug — two more were HSL artefacts.)

0

Neutral darks in 352 values

The graphite ink carrying the current design has no equivalent anywhere — nearest is Mub 1600 at ΔE 9.49, and every other dark stop is chromatic. The palette can dress the product but cannot supply its primary text color.

Working rules

Warm at 100–400, never above — and use Mub

Coffee, Cocoa and Mub are backgrounds and hairlines only; from 500 up they read as brown, and brown ink looks like a mistake next to clinical content. Of the three, Mub is the one to reach for — its hue sits at 94–97°, matching the shipped beige (93°), while Coffee and Cocoa run pinker at 68–75°. All text and borders come from Grey.

Ocean is the brand family, not Blue

Ocean 1500 is the closest single value to the shipped navy. Blue and Indigo both push violet at every step and will never sit correctly next to it.

Lighter for area, darker for meaning

Wash and Tint carry surface area — the more of the screen a color covers, the lighter it must be. UI, Text and Ink carry meaning in small amounts. A color that appears at both scales needs two different steps, not one at reduced opacity.

Yellow, Lemon, Mustard and Orange never carry text

Orange needs step 1400 to reach AA and Yellow needs 1600, at which point neither reads as the color you picked. Use them as fills with graphite or white text on top.

Skip Grey 1500 and Ocean 1000

Two broken steps, each perceptually lighter than its predecessor, so any ramp, gradient or hover state passing through one will visibly kink. Violet 1200 and Ember 1100 were originally on this list in error — measured in HSL rather than CIELAB. Both are fine; Ember 1100 is in fact the value you want for negative text on a warm surface.

Semantic color always pairs with text or an icon

The shipped success green (#4FA858) is 2.97:1 on white; its palette neighbour Green 900 is 3.12:1. Both sit in the Accent band, so neither can be the only signal that something succeeded. Same for every accent-band value.

What this doesn't do yet

This is the map, not the system. When the token work starts, in this order:

  1. Cut 22 families to 8. Grey, Coffee, Ocean, Green, Coral, Mustard, Turquoise, plus one categorical set for data. The rest is optionality nobody asked for.
  2. Re-space the ramps. Rebuild on even perceptual lightness so step numbers mean the same thing in every family — then the AA line lands in one place and semantic tokens become possible.
  3. Name by role, not by hue. surface / ink / brand / positive / negative / notice / informative, each pointing at a step. Partner theming swaps the pointer, not the component.
  4. Fold the production values in. The warm beige and graphite ink that ship today are better choices for health content than anything in this file. They should be in the palette, not beside it.
