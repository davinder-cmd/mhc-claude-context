MH Brand × MH Colors v2

How far the 2021 brand palette sits from the Uber-derived working palette, measured in ΔE2000 — and which substitutions survive a brand review.

Brand: MHC Brand Guidelines v1.2 (2021) · Palette: Figma "MH colors v2" · CIEDE2000 verified against Sharma et al. test data · warm–cool span = 161 b* units

Your four questions

Is MH Blue the same as Ocean 900?
No — not close. MH Blue is Ocean 1300 (ΔE 1.41, brand-grade). Ocean 900 is ΔE 13.1 away.
Ocean 900 is 15 L* lighter. You'd be shipping a different blue. Ocean 1200 (ΔE 2.69) is the only other stop inside a defensible tolerance.
How about the orange?
Both brand oranges are effectively exact. Lava-orange → Ember 700, ΔE 0.77. Tangerine → Orange 700, ΔE 0.62.
Below ΔE 1 is imperceptible even side by side. These are the strongest matches in the whole set — adopt them without discussion.
How about the pale green?
Weakest area. Glacier → Jade 200 at ΔE 3.69; sky-blue → Teal 400 at ΔE 4.64. Both only defensible at tertiary tier.
The palette has no pale, low-chroma green-cyan. Its light stops are all more saturated than MH's pastels. If glacier matters, keep it exact.
The secondaries?
Two of nine fail: aqua-blue (ΔE 6.0) and slate (ΔE 5.67). The rest land between ΔE 1.8 and 3.9.
Keep aqua-blue exact — it carries CTA recognition. Let slate go: its cool blue-grey is precisely what you're warming away from.

The tolerance ladder

This is what makes a substitution defensible in a brand review. Tolerance is budgeted by tier — the primary mark gets the tightest, tertiaries get latitude, because recognition load is not evenly distributed.

ΔE2000What the eye doesDefensible for
≤ 1.0Cannot see a difference, even side by sideAnything, including the logo
≤ 2.0Visible only on close side-by-side inspectionPrimary brand color — the required bar

The process, repeatable

Fix the ground truth first

One file, one date, one source. Here: Brand Guidelines v1.2 (2021). If two candidate "brand blues" exist, resolve that before measuring anything.

Measure in ΔE2000, never in hex or HSL

Hex distance and HSL both lie about perceived difference — they over-weight blues and under-weight greens. ΔE2000 is the standard brand-QA metric and is what a brand review will accept.

Budget tolerance by tier, not uniformly

Primary ≤ 2.0, secondary ≤ 3.5, tertiary ≤ 5.0. Recognition load concentrates in the primary and the lockup; spending equal rigor on a tertiary wash is wasted.

Separate brand assets from UI tokens

Logos and co-branded marks use exact values forever. UI tokens use palette stops within tolerance — that's what buys you the sibling steps, the hover states and the accessible twins.

Give every failing color an accessible twin from its own family

Most brand accents fail AA as text. The fix is not to darken the brand color — it's to pair the brand fill with a darker stop of the same hue, so the color reads as brand and the text still passes.

Verify in context, not in isolation

Albers' point, and it's measurable: the same hex on #FFFFFF and on #FAF7F0 are different contrast problems. Always re-measure against the surface the color actually sits on.

Log the exceptions with their ΔE

"We kept aqua-blue exact because the nearest stop was ΔE 6.0" is a defence. "It looked off" is not. The number is what survives the review.

What I'd take to a brand review

Twelve of fifteen brand colors have a defensible palette equivalent. Two are deliberate replacements in service of warming. One stays exact.

  1. The oranges and the primary blue are settled. ΔE 0.62, 0.77 and 1.41 — no reasonable reviewer contests these.
  2. Warming happens in the neutrals only. Cloud → Mub 200, silver → Mub 400. Ink stays neutral Grey because the warm ramps go brown below L* 55.
  3. Aqua-blue stays exact as an off-palette stop. It's a CTA color at ΔE 6.0 from anything — substituting it is the one change someone would notice.
  4. Slate is retired, not matched. Replacing a cool blue-grey with neutral Grey 800 is the warming decision, stated plainly rather than hidden inside a tolerance argument.
