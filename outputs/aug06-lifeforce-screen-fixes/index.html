LifeForce — screen fixes

Four screens: enrollment, waiting, post-enrollment score, and the results tab. Left column is the current design; right column is the proposed redesign. No scores — fixes only.

Notes

1Three representations of one four-step job

“3 of 4 done” in the bar, then the remaining step as a hero, then three completed cards. The member has to reconcile all three to answer “what’s left?”

Fix: one stepper. Four numbered rows, the current one open with its action, the finished ones struck through on a single line each. Delete the progress bar and the “Completed” section entirely — the stepper already says both.

2The screen grows as the member progresses

At 1-of-4 done, completed work is one card. At 3-of-4 it’s three cards with dates, and the screen is mostly history. Effort and screen length move in opposite directions to progress.

Fix: completed steps collapse to one line, on the recessed surface tone. Dates move behind a disclosure — nobody needs “Completed Jan 12” on the primary screen.

3The largest text on screen is a task name, not an outcome

“Health Assessment” at 20/Semibold is the visual headline. It tells the member what to click, not why or what happens after. The reason — “Then you start earning your credit” — is a 12px caption three blocks up.

Fix: headline the outcome (“One step left before Peak Health books your visit”), demote the task name to the button. Move the credit line up beside progress where it earns its keep.

4“Recommended · do this next” is two labels, and one is wrong

These steps are required to earn the credit, not recommended. And the eyebrow spends its whole budget on a fact the card’s position already communicates.

Fix: remove → STEP 4 OF 4. Says something the layout doesn’t.

5Internal vocabulary in a member-facing checklist

“Program Acknowledgment” is what the legal team calls it. The member is being asked to read and accept two documents.

Fix: “Accept program terms”, with the Company Release and Wellness Program Notice named in the body.

Redesign alternatives

A · Single stepper card shown rightAll four steps live in one card; the current step expands inline with its action. One object, one mental model, shrinks as you go.
B · Hero + collapsed historyKeep a distinct hero for the active step, collapse all completed work into one summary row. Closer to today’s structure, less rework, but keeps two objects describing one process.

A is the recommendation — it removes the duplication rather than tidying it. Choose B if the hero card is shared with the deadline states (frames 08–11) and can’t be refactored independently.

Notes

1A waiting screen with no date

“Typically within 20 business days” is the only timing signal, it’s inside a paragraph, and it’s relative. A member who finished on 12 June cannot tell you when to start worrying — so they call.

Fix: compute and show the date. “Booked by ~10 July” as a stated expectation, plus what to do if it passes. A waiting state’s primary job is to define the end of the wait.

2The one available action is labelled like an upsell

On the single screen where the member can do nothing else, the dependent invite is buried under “MAXIMIZE YOUR CREDIT” — the register of a promo, not of the next useful step.

Fix: promote it. It becomes the primary action while they wait; the credit framing moves into body copy where it’s a reason rather than a barker.

3“You’ve finished all 4 steps” is restated three times

The headline says it, the “Complete · 4/4” badge says it, and four checked rows say it again. Roughly a third of the screen is dedicated to confirming completed work.

Fix: one line, recessed. The four-item checklist collapses to “Enrollment complete · 4/4 ›”.

4Nothing prepares the member for the visit

One sentence says a nurse will “take your health measurements.” No duration, no location, no fasting guidance, nothing to bring.

Fix: add “What to expect” — three lines. Cheapest available reduction in no-shows and in the support calls that follow them.

5On frame 05, reschedule is caption text

Once a date exists, “Add to calendar” is a 16/Medium button while “Need to reschedule? Call…” is 12px grey. If the date is wrong, rescheduling is the more urgent need — and it requires a phone call, which deserves a real target.

Fix: two buttons of equal weight — Add to calendar and Call to reschedule as a tel: link.

Redesign alternatives

A · Timeline shown rightShow the whole arc — enrolled, being scheduled, visit, results — with the member’s position and a projected date. Turns dead air into a sequence with a visible end.
B · “You’re all set” + prep checklistSingle confirmation card plus what to do before the visit. Simpler and cheaper, but doesn’t answer when, which is the actual question.

A is the recommendation, because the missing information is temporal. Do B only if a projected date can’t be computed from Peak Health’s SLA — in which case state the SLA as a date range rather than a duration.

Notes

1The score is outranked by a status confirmation

“You’re active in LifeForce” is useful once, on the day it becomes true. It permanently holds the hero at 20/Semibold, while Phase 2 of 5 — the thing that sets the member’s money — sits below it at 16.

Fix: swap them. Phase becomes the hero at display size; “active” becomes a one-line eyebrow, or disappears after first view.

2Five dots don’t say which direction is good

One filled dot of five communicates position and nothing else. Is 5 the goal or the warning? Is 2 progress or a problem? The copy doesn’t resolve it either — “where your health is today” is deliberately neutral.

Fix: a segmented scale with a labelled direction and the member’s position marked. Name what the ends mean.

3The money is one tap away and never shown

Phase is only meaningful as dollars per pay period, and that number lives behind a link to a calculator. The product’s entire payoff is off-screen.

Fix: show the amount inline next to the phase. The calculator becomes a secondary link for modelling other scenarios, not the only route to the number.

4A score with no lever

Nothing on the screen says what moves the phase. Without that, the member can read their rank but can’t act on it — the definition of a vanity metric.

Fix: one line — “What moves your phase” — linking to the biometrics that determine it. That also connects this tab to the results tab, which currently has no relationship to it.

5Completed enrollment still occupies a third of the screen

Four checked rows plus a badge, months after enrollment ended. This block is identical on frames 04, 05, 07 and 12 — it never earns its space again after the wait state.

Fix: collapse to one recessed line once the member is active.

Redesign alternatives

A · Phase as hero, credit inline shown rightOne dominant stat with the scale beneath it and the dollar amount attached. Single focal point; the money is a supporting fact.
B · Two-stat heroPhase and credit side by side at equal weight. Answers both questions at once, but splits the focal point and reads as a dashboard rather than a result.

A is the recommendation — one hero, one number, everything else supporting. Choose B if research shows members come to this screen primarily to check the dollar amount rather than their health standing, in which case lead with money and demote phase.

Notes

1Range state is invisible — accessibility blocker

The member is shown a value and a goal and left to do the comparison, twelve times on the full-results screen. Three of these four are out of goal and nothing indicates it.

Fix: a word plus tone — “Above goal” / “In range”. It must be text or a mark, not colour alone; a red number would swap one accessibility failure for the same one.

2The programs and the results are unconnected

Virta is weight loss and BMI is 27.4. Those two facts are four inches apart on the same screen and nothing says one is the reason for the other. The list reads as generic marketing when it is in fact personalised care.

Fix: carry the reason on the card — “Recommended because your BMI is above goal.” Highest-value change on this screen: same content, transformed from an ad into a care plan.

3“See all recommendations & handouts” appears twice

Once after the results tiles, once after the programs list — same label, same destination, one screen.

Fix: delete one. Keep the one after the programs list, where it terminates a section rather than interrupting one.

4The blood pressure unit is broken

The frame contains stray text nodes — 1, m, m — beside 128/82. “mmHg” has fragmented into separate layers, and only BP carries a unit at all.

Fix: rebuild as one text node. Decide whether units appear at all — if not, drop them everywhere rather than on three of four tiles.

5Three decorative tints with no rule

The program icons use a green, a blue and a warm tint. None maps to a category, a status or a severity — the variation is ornamental, and it competes with the tinting that range state now needs to use.

Fix: one neutral tile tint for all programs. Reserve tinted surfaces for range state, where tone carries meaning.

6Results aren’t ordered by what matters

The four tiles are in arbitrary order, so the one in-range result (resting HR) sits alongside three that need attention.

Fix: sort out-of-range first. On the full-results screen, also de-emphasise the four unmeasured “—” tiles, which currently match real data in weight.

Redesign alternatives

A · Results-driven shown rightEach out-of-range result carries its recommended program inline. Removes the separate programs section — cause and response are one object.
B · Two sections with explicit connectorsKeep results and programs separate, but every program states which result it responds to. Less disruptive to the existing content model and to whoever owns the programs list.

A is the stronger experience and the bigger lift — it needs a mapping from biometric to program that may not exist in the data yet. B is the recommendation if that mapping is manual, since it delivers most of the value with a copy change.
