Why now

v6 retired gradients from product entirely and pulled jewel weight back to 100–200 stops on categoricals (passive editorial restraint, color-as-accent). The user's brief — warmer surfaces, brand-DNA categoricals (blue/teal/green/orange) re-engaged, Verily as primary reference, "not heavy" — pushes consistently in one direction: Active brand role at low saturation. This audit tests whether Light Active beats both the v6 baseline (potentially too cold/restrained for Phase 0 Human) and a full Verily-coded Active (potentially too chromatic for Phase 0 Credible at our scale).

Candidates

Three candidates span the editorial-restraint ↔ active-chromatic axis. All preserve R2 primary (D2), Clinical-Modern Distinctive tribe (D1), HC-3 neutral/sand split, and WCAG AA. They differ in where chroma lives — glyph only, container fill at light stops, or container fill at medium stops with multi-hue gradients.

Selection criteria

Phase 0's five attributes (Credible, Direct, Outcome-obsessed, Human, AI-competent) plus D1 Distinctive and HC-6 WCAG AA. Same criteria, every candidate. No moving the goalposts.

  1. Credible (Phase 0) — does the candidate read clinical-credible at the buyer hero presentation scale? Would a CHRO put this on a board slide without it embarrassing the brand?
  2. Direct (Phase 0) — does it stay out of the way of the message? Chromatic noise that forces the reader to filter color before reading content is a Direct failure.
  3. Outcome-obsessed (Phase 0) — is data treatment legible? Numbers, charts, percentage deltas must read instantly at member-screen scale.
  4. Human (Phase 0) — does the system feel warm, approachable, not corporate-cold? This is the attribute v6's editorial restraint risks under-serving.
  5. AI-competent (Phase 0) — does the AI surface treatment signal AI without dating fast and without leaning on indigo (per C2 deemphasize-purple)?
  6. Distinctive (D1) — does it differentiate from the B2B-wellbeing cluster (Personify/Sharecare/Thrive/Navigate) without crossing into wellness-lifestyle (Calm/Headspace)?
  7. WCAG AA (HC-6) — every text-on-background pairing ≥ 4.5:1; every UI-component edge ≥ 3:1. Verified per pairing in §A11y.

Recommendation

C2 — Light Active direction-locked

C2 wins 6/7 criteria outright (Credible, Direct, Outcome-obsessed, Human, AI-competent, Distinctive, WCAG AA — all pass). It serves Human meaningfully better than C1 without sacrificing Credible or Direct, which is the exact trade the user's brief asks for ("warmer, brand-aligned, not heavy"). The hero gradient at 100-stop and the chromatic-tinted module containers bring brand DNA forward; the deep primary.600 CTA and white-card data treatment hold the clinical-credibility floor.

Tradeoff accepted: Phase 0 Distinctive is well served but not maximally — C2 sits between the editorial-restraint distinctive lane (C1) and the chromatic-pastel distinctive lane (C3 / Verily / Calm / Lovi). The middle path means we don't fully own either signal. We accept this because both flanking positions have larger downside risks: C1 risks reading corporate-cold (Human failure), and C3 risks reading consumer-wellness (Credible failure) at our scale.

D11 status update: The locked principle "color is an accent, not a coat" amends to "color is an accent at light saturation; one chromatic moment per screen is permitted as a coat at 100-stop." Container fills at 100-stop on module identity and a single hero card per screen are the new permitted active applications. CTAs, ink, and surface backgrounds remain governed by passive editorial restraint.

Alts retained

C1 — Passive baseline · kept as fallback. If verification gate G2 (member-warmth at applied scale with real photography) fails for C2, revert to C1 + commission warmer photography to compensate. C1 is the proven safe state — v6 shipped it; we know it holds Credible and Direct without question.
C3 — Verily-coded Active · dropped. Reason: chromatic saturation that high (200-stop containers + multi-hue gradients on every category) sits closer to consumer-wellness aesthetic than Clinical-Modern Distinctive (D1). Verily can absorb that aesthetic because their buyer is researchers/care orgs in long-cycle relationships; MHC's buyer (CHRO/benefits manager evaluating for ROI) needs the credibility floor heavier. The C3 strip on the buyer hero specifically reads "consumer-app marketing" rather than "clinical platform." Retire entirely.
C2 with sand-co-led variant (not built) · documented for future iteration. If the clay+sage co-lead at applied scale starts feeling too earthy for the AI surface specifically, revert sand to co-lead and hold clay+sage as accents only. Defer until G3 (AI surface re-audit) lands.

Verification gates

C2 is direction-locked. Before D11 graduates from direction-locked to locked:

  1. G1 — Tactical review with Isabel (Visual Designer). The "one chromatic moment per screen" rule needs a designer's eye on whether the hero gradient at 100-stop reads as intended at retina/sRGB-calibrated phone scale, or whether it dissolves into "almost white" and loses the active register entirely.
  2. G2 — Member-warmth test at member's actual phone scale with real photography. Render the Bonjour Andrew home with a real member portrait + the C2 hero gradient. Test against the Phase 0 Human test phrase: "would a member screenshot this to a friend who's struggling?" If it still reads corporate-cold despite warm photography, revert to C1.
  3. G3 — AI surface re-audit. The primary.50 wash + sparkle treatment is a first attempt. Test specifically against ChatGPT-Health and Claude-Health visual canon — does our AI surface read distinctly enough that members don't confuse it with a generic notification card? If not, iterate the AI marker before D11 locks.
  4. G4 — Buyer hero stress test at presentation scale (16:9 deck + 1280px+ marketing). The primary→aqua→sage gradient must hold credibility at conference scale, not just member-phone scale. Render the buyer hero in a deck slide and a marketing landing page; test with a CHRO-equivalent reviewer (Darcy or Alex) for "would you put this on a board slide" sniff test.
  5. G5 — v7 system-preview build verification. Once gates G1–G4 pass, build v7 system-preview against the C2 amendment. Verify that the layered token system holds the new "100-stop hero + 100-stop module container" rule without regressing on HC-3 (neutral/sand split), HC-6 (WCAG AA), or HC-8 (gradients on text/borders rejected).
  6. G6 — Stakeholder reaction check (Darcy, Alex). Does the brand-DNA reactivation land as "honoring 2021 brand without being beholden to it" or as "regression to corporate-medical"? The user's stated intent is the former — verify it reads that way to leadership before locking.
