Premise

Tone-on-tone is the most architecturally aggressive answer to the warm + blue muddle that v5 partially resolved with the neutral-jewel split. By collapsing the system to a single hue family, the muddle disappears by construction — but categorical identity (5 module hues), the sand/neutral split (HC-3), and the multi-hue brand architecture all collapse with it. This audit asks: can any single hue family carry the system without breaking the locked tribe and Phase 0 attributes?

Selection criteria

Six criteria, declared up front, applied identically to every candidate. The first criterion (muddle resolution) is the audit's premise. The next four trace to Phase 0 attributes and locked decisions. The last is the AA accessibility floor that bounds every candidate regardless of register.

  1. 1. Resolves the muddle. Single-hue eliminates warm/cool conflict by construction. Pass criterion: no two competing hue families coexist on a surface. (All five candidates pass by definition — the differentiator is whether the chosen family is workable on its own merits.)
  2. 2. Clinical-Modern Distinctive (D1 / HC-1). The candidate must read clinical-modern at the buyer-hero scale and member-screen scale. Not lifestyle, not consumer-wellness. Buyer-readable and member-readable simultaneously.
  3. 3. Phase 0 — Human (HC-12). Carries warmth without lifestyle softness. Member moments don't read cold or sterile. The dual-audience test: a CHRO can put it on a board slide AND a member can screenshot it to a struggling friend.
  4. 4. Phase 0 — Credible (HC-12). Buyer-side legibility. Reads as a category-leading B2B health platform, not a wellness lifestyle app, not a consumer AI startup, not a corporate intranet.
  5. 5. AI-competent (HC-12) preserved. The cross-cutting AI surface treatment from v5 (indigo wash + tag) survives or has a working substitute. The AI moment must remain visibly labeled inside the chosen monochrome.
  6. 6. WCAG AA holds (HC-6). Every text-on-bg pairing ≥ 4.5:1; every UI element edge ≥ 3:1. Tighter inside a single hue family, especially mid-stops on light surfaces.

Recommendation

T2 — Primary (R2 sky-teal bridge) tone-on-tone direction-locked

Why T2 wins. Three of the five candidates require reopening D2 (primary hue family). T2 is the only candidate that honors the locked R2 direction — the audit ahead of it (decisions/D2-primary-hue/) does not need to be redone. T2 also clears the strongest pair of criteria — Clinical-Modern Distinctive (D1) and Credible (Phase 0) — at the buyer-hero scale where category positioning is hardest to recover.

Tradeoff accepted. Phase 0 Human attribute weakens. Without sand surfaces or warm accent, the cool register risks reading "corporate medical" — the failure mode D2 was originally designed to escape. Compensation strategy: lean photography and typography hard on warmth (per v4 Rule 6), and re-evaluate if verification gates show member moments reading cold at applied scale. T1 (Warm) is held as the documented fallback for that scenario.

What this unlocks. A v6 system-preview can now build on T2 — single primary-family ladder across surfaces, accents, dividers, ink. Categorical-badge architecture (Q13) collapses; the 5 module hues (moss, lavender, rose, fuchsia, warm) retire from product, surviving only as data-viz palette and as legacy reference. The cross-cutting AI marker (indigo) needs a new treatment — see verification gate G3.

Alts retained

T1 — Warm tone-on-tone · kept as fallback. Promote to main if T2's verification gate G2 (member home warmth at applied scale) fails — i.e., if the cool register reads cold even with photography compensating. Document at alts/v6-alt-warm-tonal/ if a v6 lineage progresses.

T4 — Neutral tone-on-tone · kept as fallback. Promote if Distinctive becomes the priority over Human (e.g., if stakeholder review surfaces a "we're indistinguishable from competitors" failure mode). The most editorial-restrained option; ages best.

T3 — Moss tone-on-tone · dropped. Reason: routes to wellness-lifestyle tribe (D1 violation). Cannot proceed without first reopening D1, which is locked. Re-evaluate only if D1 itself reopens.

T5 — Indigo tone-on-tone · dropped. Reason: collapses the AI cross-cutting marker (Phase 0 §9 deliverable — when the whole system is indigo, the AI surface no longer stands out). Sits adjacent to HC-5 (purple-as-primary) at the deepest stops. The strategic premise (AI-native brand) is real but better served by an AI-surface-treatment iteration on top of T2, not by collapsing the brand into the AI marker.

Lavender tone-on-tone · pre-excluded from candidate set. Reason: would trigger a second hard-constraint reopen (HC-5: purple is categorical only). Including it would have required a parallel decision-probe to reopen Q10. Out of scope for D12.

Verification gates

Before D12 graduates from direction-locked to locked, and before a v6 system-preview can land on T2 as its register baseline:

  1. G1 — Tactical review with Isabel (Visual Designer). Walk T2 applied moments at production fidelity. Confirm that primary tone-on-tone reads as a deliberate restraint move, not as a system that lost its categorical hues.
  2. G2 — Member home warmth test. Render the v6 home moment (Bonjour Andrew or equivalent) at a member's actual phone scale, with real photography. Decide whether the cool register reads warm-enough or whether T1 (warm) needs to be promoted. This is the single highest-risk gate for T2.
  3. G3 — AI surface re-treatment audit. Define how the cross-cutting AI marker survives inside a primary-only system. Options to evaluate: (a) re-introduce indigo as a single categorical accent for AI only — explicit exception to tone-on-tone; (b) replace hue-cue with typographic / iconographic treatment; (c) use deepest primary stops (900–950) as the AI surface anchor. Lock one before v6 builds.
  4. G4 — Buyer hero stress test at presentation scale. Render the hero on a 16:9 deck and on the marketing site at 1280px+. Confirm primary-only deep gradient reads as category-leading B2B-health, not as a generic SaaS template.
  5. G5 — Stakeholder reaction check (Darcy, Alex). Does corporate-brand alignment land at full tone-on-tone? The legacy Brand Blue (#0F497F) and Aqua Blue (#04A0B7) both descend from this family, so T2 should strengthen alignment — confirm at executive review.
  6. G6 — Build-time AA pairing audit at v6. Verify the borders-move-200-to-300 rule lands across every component when built into the system, not just the hero pilot. Especially critical for selection containers, focus rings, and disabled states.

When all six gates pass, D12 graduates to locked and Q13 (categorical-badge architecture) is formally retired. D2 remains direction-locked on R2 — its own verification gates (in decisions/D2-primary-hue/audit.html §7) are independent of D12.
