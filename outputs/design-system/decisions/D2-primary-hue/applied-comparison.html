What you can see in the applied-UI that you couldn't see at the swatch level

  • R1 reads warmer than expected on the buyer hero. The teal-aqua heritage shows; some stakeholders may read it as "wellness vendor" rather than "clinical platform." Best for member-side moments; weakest for buyer-side.
  • R2 lands quietly in all three. Doesn't dominate any single screen; doesn't fail any of the five attributes. Most rounded performance — which is exactly what a system primary should do.
  • R3 dominates the buyer hero but cools the AI Insight. The deep navy gradient is the most premium-clinical of the three at scale. The AI tag in the AI Insight reads more "enterprise software" than "warm care." Best for buyer-side; weakest for member-side.

Recommendation

R2 — Sky-teal bridge — direction stands.

The applied-UI comparison confirms the swatch-level lock. R2 is the only candidate that performs at parity across all three audiences — not the warmest (R1 wins Human), not the most authoritative (R3 ties on Credible/AI-competent), but the only one that holds all five attributes simultaneously without trading one for another.

If verification fails on the warmth read (member finds R2 too cool): pivot to R1.
If verification fails on the authority read (CHRO finds R2 not premium-clinical enough): pivot to R3.

Direct comparison takeaway

  • R1 wins the member moment in isolation; loses the buyer hero.
  • R3 wins the buyer hero in isolation; loses the member moment warmth.
  • R2 doesn't win any single moment, but doesn't lose any either. For a B2B2C platform that needs the same screen to read to both audiences, that's the right tradeoff.
