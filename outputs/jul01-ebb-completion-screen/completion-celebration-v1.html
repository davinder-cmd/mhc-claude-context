Read the screen top to bottom — each zone maps to a decision we locked. The order is deliberate: the health win comes before the money, and the next path comes right after both.

Top · the celebration

Lead with the health win, not the money

We name the accomplishment first — the topic and the real work done ("all 10 sessions"). The member should feel capable and proud before a dollar figure appears. This is what makes the behavior stick after the reward is gone.

The $100, then how much more is on the table

The reward is stated plainly and given the headline slot — it's our bread and butter. Right below it, the "$100 of $500 · this year" meter shows there are four more paths worth $400 still ahead. That's the quiet pull toward the next loop.

Next-up · tee up the next path

One clear next, drawn from their interests

This is the highest-momentum moment to start the next path, so we make it effortless: a single recommended topic pulled from what they told us they care about, with its own "$100" tag, and a primary button to begin. The whole loop turns right here.

Bottom · the escape hatches

Strongly suggested — never forced

"Browse all care paths" and "Not now — back to home" keep it honest and autonomy-supportive. We point hard at one next path, but the member is always free to choose another or step away. A locked door would cost us the people who stall.

Variants to design next

This is the everyday completion. The same layout flexes to a few important edge states:

First-ever completion

An extra beat — this is a bigger moment. "Your first care path. Here's your first $100." First-time moments punch above their weight.

Cap reached (5 of 5)

The meter is full. We stop dangling payout and switch the tee-up to health framing — "You've earned the max this year; here's what's worth doing next anyway."

No strong next match

When interests don't point to an obvious next path, the tee-up softens to "Explore care paths" rather than forcing a weak recommendation.
