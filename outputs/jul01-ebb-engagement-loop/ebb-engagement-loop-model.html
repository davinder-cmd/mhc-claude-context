Where the loop lives

Five trigger surfaces

The loop isn't a screen — it re-enters from five places. Each one shows progress and reward, framed around the health goal.

Home

Hero: recommended path with "$100 reward." A slim cap meter — "$300 of $500 this year." Keep-going for in-progress paths.

Topic home

"Your progress · Session 3 of 7" with distance to the $100. No social clutter — progress and the next step lead.

Rewards

EBB leads the page: $ earned / $500 cap and the next opportunity. Points, store, and raffles stay — but secondary.

Push notification

Behavior-timed, honest, health-first: "Two sessions left" — finish Managing Depression to earn $100 and sleep better.

In-app notification

Completion alerts, gentle nudges, and incentive confirmations — always tied to real program state, never blasted on a fixed schedule.

The rule for all five

Never a reward cue without a progress cue, and never either without the health reason behind it.

Decisions · locked 2026-07-01

What we've decided

Each call below is locked, with the reasoning and the option we ruled out. All of it is kept in a running decision log (projects/feature-ebb/_decisions.md) so every choice stays defensible — and easy to revisit if feedback pushes back.

D1
✓ Decided

When someone finishes a program, how do we handle what they do next?

The whole point of EBB is to keep people moving from one program to the next. The question is how much we push versus how much freedom we give.

Lock them to one at a time Ruled out

They can only have one program open, and must finish it before starting another. Very focused — but if they get stuck on one they don't like, they're cornered and may just quit.

Point to one clear "next" — but let them browse Chosen

One obvious "do this next" front and center, so there's always a clear thing to start — with the full list of programs one tap away if they'd rather choose something else.

Let them juggle several at once Ruled out

An open shelf — start as many as they like. Most freedom, but the pull to finish one and move to the next gets weak. It becomes a menu, not a path.

Why: people finish more when there's an obvious next step — but a locked door backfires. Someone stuck on a program they dislike, with no other option, tends to abandon the app entirely. Make the next step obvious, never forced.

Decided — point to one clear next; keep the full library one tap away.

D2
✓ Decided

When do we celebrate — and how big?

Today the app throws confetti when someone starts and says "earn 1,000 points" — wrong moment, wrong currency. We move the big celebration to the finish, and set a ladder so each rung is louder than the last and the payout stays the peak.

Start a topicBrief, warm, non-monetary "attaboy" — "You're taking a real step for your health. Let's go." (Extra-warm the first time.)
Lesson · ~40–60Silent — the progress bar advances, no celebration. Too frequent to celebrate without becoming noise.
Session · ~7–12Modest micro-moment — "Session 3 of 8 done, closer to $100." The halfway point gets a nod.
Topic completeThe peak + the payout: health win → "You earned $100 · 1 of 5 this year" → next topic teed up. First-ever completion gets an extra beat.

Why: escalating loudness keeps completion the unmistakable high point; celebrating frequent lessons would cheapen the $100 and add friction. Finishing is the emotional peak and the best moment to hand off the next topic.

Decided — celebrate the finish; silent lessons; session micro-moments; non-monetary start.

D3
✓ Decided

How does the $100 sit next to the other rewards?

The rewards page already shows several kinds of rewards — points, store credit, raffle entries. The $100 for finishing a program is new, and it's a bigger deal. Does it blend in, or does it lead?

Blend it in with the rest Ruled out

The $100 is just one more reward type alongside points, store credit, and raffles. Nothing stands out.

Make the $100 the headline Chosen

The $100 leads the rewards page and the home screen. Points, store credit, and raffles still exist — they just sit below it, not competing for attention.

Why: buried among five reward types, the $100 loses its punch. One clear, big, easy-to-understand reward — "finish a program, get $100" — beats a cluttered shelf of small ones. This is EBB's bread and butter; it earns the headline.

Decided — EBB is the headline; points, store, and raffles stay but secondary.

D4
✓ Decided

How many topics can someone pick — and does "focus" mean one topic?

A single-topic "focus" model was floated for performance reasons. But EBB deliberately loads members with several programs across topics — and the daisy chain needs somewhere to go next.

Single-topic focus Ruled out

The member commits to one topic; home is built around it. Simple and light — but it caps completions at one topic and leaves the chain nowhere to go. Works against EBB.

Multi-topic interest — pick 2 or more Chosen

Members pick several interests up front (onboarding already says "select all that apply"). "Focus" survives only as a display choice — surface one clear next action — never a single-topic commitment.

Why: the chain needs a pool to pull the next program from, and EBB wants members completing up to 5 topics a year. Performance is a rendering problem — one hero image, lean sections, lazy-load — not a reason to narrow the product. The home-redesign work already reached the same conclusion.

Decided — multi-topic capture (2+); "focus" is display-only, never single-topic.

Guardrails

What keeps this a health loop, not a slot machine

Drawn from the engagement-loop research and reference/engagement-behavior-experts.md — the evidence is clear that in health, mechanics alone backfire.

Reward is an accelerant, not the motor

Pair every $ cue with the health "why." Expected money can crowd out intrinsic motivation — the #1 risk both research passes flagged.

Gamification stays light

Reminders and human/coach contact are the proven retainers in health apps; points and streaks are marginal and can even hurt retention.

The path actually teaches

Progress toward competence and better health, not just completion clicks — so the benefit outlasts the payment.

Cap gracefully

At $500 / 5 completed, stop dangling payout. Shift the loop to health progress and mastery framing rather than a reward that can't be paid.
