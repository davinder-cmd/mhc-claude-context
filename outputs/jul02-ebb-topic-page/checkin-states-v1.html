"You've been feeling better" is only ever shown when the data earns it. The block is state-aware — it reflects the member's actual assessment trend, stays humble when there isn't enough data, and turns supportive (never chipper) when things aren't improving.

The state above the states

When it's a safety event, not a UI variant

🛟

A worsening score past the safety threshold routes to crisis/escalation

On a mental-health path, a decline past a clinically-defined safety threshold isn't a card state — it hands off to the DCP's existing crisis-routing (the same architecture that already handles self-harm, suicidal ideation, etc., bypassing the LLM). The "Needs support" card handles the ordinary flat/harder weeks; the safety path handles the serious ones. Both must exist.

Needs clinical sign-off

Three things clinical owns, not design

The thresholds

What score change counts as "improving," "no meaningful change," and "safety." Design renders these; clinical sets them.

The copy for each state

Especially the "Needs support" language and anything implying a health claim — reviewed for tone and accuracy.

The escalation trigger

Exactly when the block gives way to crisis-routing, and what that hand-off looks like.

Principle: the product only ever claims improvement when the assessment supports it; worsening trends route to support (or crisis), never to cheerful copy. This applies anywhere the experience implies health improvement — including the completion celebration. States v1 — for clinical review + reaction.
