Continue card · the one primary action

Resume exactly where they left off

The screen opens on the single next action — "Continue session 3," with what it involves and how long. One dominant, thumb-reachable button; everything else is quieter. A returning member never has to hunt for "what now."

Recommendations · through the filters

What I'd change or watch

The design above is solid on the heuristics; these are the calls worth making explicitly.

1

Keep the $100 to one appearance on this screen

Reward at the progress line only — not on the Continue card or every session row. Repetition turns a health screen into a money meter and risks crowding out intrinsic motivation.

Nielsen #8 · Aesthetic & minimalist
2

Chunk the path — never show all 10 sessions expanded

Show completed + current + the next locked step, then "See all 10." Ten open rows blows past working memory and buries the one action that matters.

Miller's Law (7±2)
3

Make "Continue" feel instant

Optimistically open the session on tap (skeleton while it loads) rather than spinning — this is the most-tapped control on the screen, so latency here costs the most.

Doherty Threshold (<400ms)
4

Decide whether topic-level data earns its place

"Mood this week" is motivating for behavioral topics but may be noise on others (or redundant with the tracker). Recommend: show it only when the data is topic-relevant; otherwise drop it and let the path lead. Test before committing.

Cognitive Load · Tesler's Law
5

One high-emphasis action, always

Continue is the only filled button; "See all," "Log today," and rows stay low-emphasis. Guard against a second primary button creeping in — that's the muddle we just fixed on the completion screen.
