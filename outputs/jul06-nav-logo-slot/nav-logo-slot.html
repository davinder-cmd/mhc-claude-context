One logo slot for every client — sizing, then the neutral plate

Two problems are tangled together: how big a logo renders, and how it looks on a themed bar. They need different fixes. Solve sizing with one bounding box; solve appearance with a neutral plate — and drop per-client theming entirely.

1 Sizing — one box, scale to fit

The bar has a fixed height. Give the logo one fixed bounding box inside it and let every logo scale down to fit — filling to whichever dimension it hits first. No square/wide variants, no per-client buckets.

The whole rule: object-fit: contain inside a box with max-height: 30px and max-width: 168px, centered. One slot absorbs the entire aspect-ratio range.

2 Appearance — the neutral plate

Don't theme the bar to the logo — that's manual, fragile, and still can't handle mixed-color marks. Instead, drop the logo on a small white plate. The client theme shows around it; the logo always sits on a predictable surface. Same plate on every tenant — zero per-client color work.

3 Why not float the logo directly?

This is exactly the failure you flagged. A logo's baked-in color only works on one background — so on a themed bar it either disappears or fights the color. The plate is what removes the per-client tuning.
