# Editorial + crop compliance audit

*July 27, 2026 · Davinder*
*All 276 files in `projects/imagery` audited against `Photography.md` (brand statement, 8 rules) and `design/foundation/image-sizing.md` (container mechanics).*

**Evidence:** [crop-test-v1.html](crop-test-v1.html) — every file rendered into all four containers with real `object-fit: cover`; the browser does the crop. [audit-metrics.csv](audit-metrics.csv) — measured exposure, contrast, saturation, cast, colour-band concentration per file. [audit.py](audit.py) / [croptest.py](croptest.py) — re-runnable.

---

## Verdict in one paragraph

The library is **compliant on subject matter and colour discipline, and badly non-compliant on lighting and composition.** 124 of 276 files have blown highlights over 4% of the frame and 86 are high-key overall — a direct hit on rule 4's "keep exposure even," and it is why so much of the set reads as "cinematic," which rule 4 forbids by name. Separately, **56 files (20%) break when cropped into a full-bleed hero or page header** — not because the files are wrong but because the brand rule "centred focal point" is being read horizontally when the crop that destroys images is vertical. Two of the eight brand rules also directly contradict what the product needs; those are flagged at the bottom rather than treated as failures.

## 1 · Aspect ratio

The container system uses `object-fit: cover`, so file ratio isn't a pass/fail — it decides **how much of the frame gets thrown away**.

| Ratio | Files | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 3:2 (1.50–1.51) | 204 | Native. Zero loss in the split hero and editorial card |
| 1.53–1.58 | 11 | Negligible — 2–5% trimmed |
| 1.67 / 1.78 / 1.90–1.91 | 12 | Loses 10–21% of width in a 3:2 box. Check the crop before placing |
| 1.35 | 3 | Loses 10% of height in a 3:2 box |
| 0.80 | 2 | Near-square. Wide containers cut ~47% |
| **0.67 portrait** | **12** | **#11 #12 #127 #129 #157 #190 #197 #200 #202 #219 #248 #253** |

**Portrait files are the only true ratio violation.** A 4:1 hero keeps 16.7% of a 0.67 frame; a 6:1 page header keeps 11%. They are thumbnail/1:1 assets or they need re-cropping from originals. Eight of the twelve are also in the crop-failure list below.

Ratio doesn't break the **integer-dimension rule** — that's a container concern, not a file concern. Locking the container (600×400, 240dp tall) keeps output integer regardless of source ratio.

## 2 · Subject-matter placement — the real crop failure

Retained fraction under `object-fit: cover`, from a 3:2 source, narrowest shape to widest.
**Reviewed by eye at 1:1 and 4:1** — the tightest crop on each axis. The rest is inferred
monotonically, which is sound because each shape's crop is a strict superset of the tighter one
on the same axis and shares its centre: survive 4:1 and you survive 3:1, 2:1, 16:9, 16:10;

### The square crop fails differently — and on the opposite files

**Correction to my first pass.** I originally reported zero failures at 1:1 and 4:3. That came from an edge-energy concentration metric I later discarded as unreliable, and it was wrong. Rendering all 276 square crops and reviewing them gives **7 hard failures and 27 marginal**:

- **Fail:** `#1 #14 #15 #45 #85 #112 #126` — the subject sits to one side and gets cut out, leaving an empty beach, an empty office, a boardwalk with no runner on it.
- **Marginal:** `#10 #34 #38 #48 #51 #71 #91 #99 #109 #113 #146 #151 #153 #154 #155 #163 #165 #168 #173 #187 #193 #196 #200 #234 #262 #269 #276` — mostly groups losing a member: four people become three, a yoga class becomes two mats, a family of five becomes four.

**The two failure sets are almost disjoint.** 50 files fail only the wide crop, 6 fail only the square crop, and exactly one (#85) fails both. There is no single "safe" shortlist — safety is per shape, which is why verdicts are now stored per shape.

**And the two pull against each other:**

| Crop class | n | Compromised at 1:1 |
|---|---|---|
| **H — hero-capable** | 41 | **37%** (4 fail, 11 marginal) |
| T — tile / card | 203 | 9% |

Hero-capable means a small off-centre subject with clean negative space — precisely what a square avatar crop throws away. **Buying "more hero-capable photography" makes the avatar problem worse**, and vice versa. Treat them as two briefs, not one.

**Root cause, and the rule that should replace the current one.** `Photography.md` says *"Centered focal point for subject matter for cropping purposes."* The library obeys it horizontally far better than vertically — photographers centre the **body**, which puts the face in the top third, and every wide container crops from the **vertical** centre. But "centred" can't be the whole answer either, because a rigidly centred subject is what makes a photograph useless as a full-bleed hero with type on it. The rule needs both bounds, stated separately:

> **The face, or primary focal point, sits between 31% and 69% of frame height** — the band a 4:1 crop preserves (37.5–62.5% for the 6:1 page header) — **and within the middle two-thirds horizontally**, which is what a 1:1 avatar keeps.
>
> Photographs deliberately composed for a full-bleed hero — subject at one third, negative space for type — are exempt from the horizontal bound and are **tagged hero-only**. They must not be used as avatars or square thumbnails.

Those two sentences are testable on a contact sheet at buy time and would have caught all 62.

**Also note the contradiction inside rule 3.** "Centred focal point for cropping" and "use negative space / don't use rigidly centred shots" are opposite instructions, and they serve the two hero patterns in opposite ways: the **split hero never crops**, so centred is right; the **full-bleed hero with overlay text** needs an off-centre subject and clean space for type. Don't try to make one library serve both. Keep centred as the library default and treat full-bleed-with-text as a small purpose-shot set — which is what `image-sizing.md` already implies by preferring the split hero.

## 4 · Two places the brand statement blocks the product

Worth resolving deliberately rather than letting each surface improvise.

**Rule 1 vs Conditions / DCP.** "DON'T show medical settings, illness, or treatment" plus "don't portray people as patients" removes the entire vocabulary that Conditions and DCP entry points need — the honest *why you'd start* moment. The library reflects this perfectly: exactly **one** symptom image (#203) and **five** care images, all of which technically violate the rule. If DTx is the strategic priority, rule 1 needs an explicit carve-out — something like *"non-clinical depictions of symptom experience are permitted on Conditions and DCP entry surfaces; clinical settings remain out of bounds"* — otherwise those pages keep using generic-happy imagery that undercuts their own message.

**Rule 7 vs Rewards / completion.** "DON'T use forced positivity" invalidates ~28 files, and they are nearly the whole Milestone set (30 files) that rewards, completion screens and earned-badge Events draw from. Either the milestone vocabulary shifts to quiet earned satisfaction — a look, a pause, a hand on a rail — or rule 7 gets a carve-out for the moment of completion. My recommendation is the former: quiet is more credible and it is what the rest of the brand statement asks for.

## 5 · What to do

**Immediate (no purchase needed)**
1. Add `crop:hero-safe` as a hard gate — only the 41 hero-capable files, and only after the 31–69% face-band check. Everything else is locked out of full-bleed and page-header slots.
2. Prefer the **split hero** (600×400) for member surfaces. It crops nothing, so 204 files become eligible and the whole failure class disappears.
3. Reclass the 12 portrait files as thumbnail-only.
4. Retire the earlier list (#81 #114 #136 #270) plus the rule-8 breaches #110 and #146 from member surfaces.
5. Restrict #96 #164 #170 #175 #201 to clinical/admin contexts, not member home.

**Rewrite in `Photography.md`**
6. Replace "centred focal point" with the **31–69% vertical face band**, plus the horizontal centre-two-thirds for 1:1.
7. Add an exposure ceiling — no more than ~2% of pixels above 245, mean luminance under 170. This is the single most-violated rule (124 files) and the easiest to specify.
8. Add "no visible device UI in frame."
9. Add "no fine stripes or high-frequency patterns" — moiré at thumbnail sizes, not just a styling preference.
10. Resolve the two conflicts in section 4 explicitly.

**Buying brief for the next batch**
Mid-key, diffused light, no flare. Face inside the middle third vertically. Wide enough to leave clean space on one side for type. Environmental over portrait. That set is what we're actually short of — 41 hero-capable files is the ceiling on every page we design.
