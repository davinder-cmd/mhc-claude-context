Rewards display · top-anchored strips · v3

Five full-width minimal strips that sit at the top of the home page, immediately below the top nav (Personify-style persistent rewards bar). Each variation isolates one design move — points-only, multi-type lozenges, hero-points + secondary inline, progress-bar emphasis, action-prompt habits-footer style. Each is shown with a simulated nav above and a placeholder for what would sit below.

S12

Points-only minimal strip

The most minimal Personify-classic. Points balance + tier + thin progress bar to next tier + dashboard link. Other reward types are not shown — they live on the dashboard. Single-currency frame at strip scale.

What it adds

Maximum compactness. Singular focus on the points engine + tier progress. Doesn't duplicate what the rewards section deeper in the page does — the strip is a wayfinding cue ("here's where you stand"), not the full summary.

Risks

Hides the multi-type configurability moat at strip scale. If the dashboard has all four types but the strip only mentions points, the moat is invisible until a member clicks through. Best paired with a deeper rewards section on home that does the multi-type work.

S13

Multi-type lozenge strip

All four reward types as inline lozenges in a single strip — gift card, points (with tier), raffle entries, store credit. Each pill is family-tinted (warm/primary/lavender/moss) and tappable into the dashboard for that type. Multi-type story stays visible at strip scale.

What it adds

Multi-type story is visible immediately at the top of every page. Each pill is its own tap target. Configurability moat readable at a glance. Compact — same vertical footprint as a points-only strip.

Risks

Pushes chromatic budget — four families (warm + primary + lavender + moss) appear at the top of every page on every visit. v6 normally avoids that. Pills wrap to second line on narrow widths; tier progress isn't shown, only the count.

S14

Hero points + secondary inline

Big points number on the left with progress bar to next tier. To the right, a smaller block: "Also earned: $75 gift · 12 raffle · $25 store · See all rewards →". Honors the user's specific spec: points hero + smaller link about other reward types. Multi-type acknowledged but quieter than the points hero.

What it adds

Hierarchy. Points + progress is the headline; multi-type is acknowledged but doesn't compete. Members who care about the points engine (most) get it big; members who care about the gift card or raffle get it inline + a link. Closest to the user's specific suggestion.

Risks

Tallest strip of the five — needs ~88px to fit the hero number + progress + secondary list. Sticky behavior would consume ~13% of a 1200×800 viewport. Could lose the hero treatment on scroll (collapse to S12 minimal) but that's eng cost.

S15

Progress bar emphasis · tier ticker

The strip is mostly the progress bar. Tier markers at start (Silver) and end (Gold), with the current points position as a moving dot. Goal-Gradient effect at strip scale — every page load reminds you how close you are. Other reward types as small grayscale micro-pills.

What it adds

Progress is the dominant visual. Goal-Gradient activated at the most prominent surface on the page. The dot creeping toward Gold is something members notice across visits. Other types stay in the conversation as micro-pills, not promoted but not hidden.

Risks

If the member is just below a tier threshold, the bar is constantly close-to-full and may feel gameplay-y rather than informative. For low-engagement members the bar barely moves week-to-week — could feel stagnant. Three-zone label (start/current/end) gets tight on narrow widths and may need to drop the start/end labels on mobile.

S16

Action-prompt strip · habits-footer style

Borrows the Healthy Habits footer line directly: "+30 pts available today · Streak: 12 days · Current 1,250 pts · Earn more →". Subtle warm background (sand-100) so the strip differentiates from the main page surface (sand-50). Frames the rewards strip as what you can do, not what you have.

What it adds

Behavioral pull at strip scale. Frames every page load around what's available to earn TODAY, not what's already in the bank. The streak is a Goal-Gradient hook ("don't break it"). Subtle sand-100 wash differentiates the strip from the page without being loud.

Risks

If "available today" is not reliably populated (no data on what to do, no smart engine), the strip reads broken. Reads more like a notification banner than a balance — clients who lead with "you've earned $75" framing may feel the value gets buried. Streak logic must handle missed days gracefully (don't reset to 0 publicly).
