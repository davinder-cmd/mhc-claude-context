Rewards display · minimal footprint v4

Five more variations in the same minimal-footprint pattern as S14 (hero number + supplementary aside). Each holds the same content — 1,250 pts · Silver · 750 to Gold · $75 gift card · 12 raffle entries · $25 store credit · "View rewards dashboard" — and varies only the layout treatment.

S17

Reversed — secondary left, hero right

Same content as the reference, but the columns swap. The "ALSO EARNED" aside sits on the left; points hero sits on the right. Tests if right-aligned hero feels like a destination (an end-state to reach) rather than the starting point of a scan.

What it adds

Right-side hero is the "rest position" of the eye after scanning — places the destination number where reading finishes, not where it starts. The aside leads instead of trailing, which can read as setup before payoff.

Risks

Reverses the conventional left-to-right hierarchy most members expect. The CTA ("View rewards dashboard") sits in the left aside which can feel premature — members haven't seen the headline yet. Mobile collapses to a stack where order matters more.

S18

Multi-tier dot progression

Progress bar shows the full tier journey — Bronze ● Silver ● Gold ● Platinum — with the current position marked by a halo. Reduces "next-tier-only" anxiety and hints at what's ahead beyond Gold. Goal-Gradient effect across the whole arc.

What it adds

Full-journey context. Members see they're past Bronze, at Silver, with Gold and Platinum ahead. The current dot's halo makes the "you are here" obvious without text. Goal-Gradient activates across multiple horizons, not just the immediate next milestone.

Risks

Tier names need to exist as a proper system in the back-end (Bronze/Silver/Gold/Platinum thresholds and per-client variations). For low-engagement members, the current dot barely moves and the bar shows mostly-empty distance to Gold — could feel like "you're far from where you should be." 4-tier systems may not match every client's reward ladder.

S19

One-line ticker — most stripped

Most extreme minimal footprint. Single horizontal row in one card — eyebrow, points, tier, progress bar, "also earned" inline, dashboard link. No big hero number, no separate aside. Behaves like an info ticker. Lowest visual weight on the home page; highest density per pixel.

What it adds

Lightest footprint possible while keeping all the data. Suits a home page where rewards isn't the primary surface (other sections need the visual weight). Fits cleanly into a sticky strip if you want it persistent.

Risks

No visual moment — points balance reads as a stat in a row, not a celebration. On narrow desktop / wide mobile the row wraps awkwardly into 2-3 lines. Could feel undervalued for clients who lead with rewards engagement metrics. Test how the "Also: gift / raffle / store" reads when individually $0 (e.g., points-only client).

S20

Single column · inline secondary

No aside box. Hero on top (eyebrow + points + tier + progress), then a thin divider, then the "also earned" block as a single low-key line of inline text + dashboard link. Reads as one cohesive moment with the secondary tucked beneath, not split across two columns.

One column reading. Hero gets the full width — bigger emotional impact for the points number. Secondary still appears but doesn't fight for column space. Single-card identity stays cohesive at any breakpoint; no awkward stacking when the aside drops.

S21

Tier-badge hero

The tier becomes the visual hero — a circular badge with "SILVER" prominently rendered. Points sit smaller next to it. The badge gives the tier identity (something members can show off) without needing the full progression bar to tell the story. Identity-first rather than balance-first.

What it adds

Tier identity is now a visual artifact — a circular badge members might mentally collect (Silver → Gold → Platinum). Personalizes the rewards moment without needing copy to do the work. Translates well to share/celebration moments (badge could become a sticker, screenshot-friendly).

Risks

Pushes toward gamification — circles + tier names + gradient badges are aesthetics consumer-fitness apps use. Members may parse it as "level / status" rather than "rewards earned." For populations who don't engage with tier mechanics, the badge is dead weight. The gradient on the badge departs from v6's gradient retirement (B4) — would need a flat treatment alternative or an explicit exception.
