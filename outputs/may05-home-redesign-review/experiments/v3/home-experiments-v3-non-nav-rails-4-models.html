MHC Home — v3 · 4 Non-Nav Rail Concepts calm + scannable

v3 (continued) · 2026-05-07 · Block fidelity · Desktop 1200 + Mobile 375

Four conceptual models for what a non-navigation right rail could hold. Companion to v3's 7 sidebar variants — those explored sidebar shape (left/right/icon/dismissible/triple); these explore what content lives in the rail when the rail is non-nav. Status (3.B) and Engagement (3.E) were two such concepts; below are four more.

All four use the same shell as 3.B/3.E: top nav handles wayfinding, right rail (200px) holds the conceptual model. Boxed hero in the main content column. v2 prominence ranking + calm/scannable rules carry forward from earlier work.

What's locked across all 4 variants:
  • Top nav (logo center, separate spec)
  • 200px right rail with one conceptual model populating it
  • Boxed hero — never full-bleed; sits as a card within the content column
  • v2 ranking — Hero w5, Things I care about + From your employer w4 paired, Programs DCP-only w3, Tracker+Insights paired w3
  • Calm rendering — 1–2 items per content section, soft fills, generous gaps, single CTA
  • Mobile reflow — rail content folds to top of content area; sidebar collapses to hamburger
What varies: the rail's conceptual model.

VAR 3.H

Goals & Progress Rail

Conceptual model: the rail shows what you're working toward. Each goal is an aspirational target with a progress bar, days remaining, and the reward tied to completion. Goals can be member-set (custom) or system-suggested (HRA-driven). Tests: does the Goal-Gradient effect (motivation increases as you approach a goal — UX laws ref) translate into measurable home-page return engagement when goals are persistently visible?

Rail contents

  • 3–4 goals shown at once · top: most-progressed (closest to completion) · "+ Add" affordance at top
  • Each goal: name, progress bar, days remaining (or sessions remaining), reward tied to completion
  • Empty state: "Set your first goal" prompt; can pull from HRA-recommended goals
  • Goal types: time-bound outcomes (sleep by date, weight by month), streaks (10K steps for 30 days), program-tied (DCP graduation)

When this wins · risks

Wins when: goals are a primary engagement lever for the population · HRA + DCP coupling supports auto-suggesting goals · Goal-Gradient (Cialdini, Yablonski) is real in the data · members value seeing their progress at every page load.

Risks: goals require a goal-engine that doesn't fully exist today · members without goals see a "set goals" empty state on every page · stale goals (set in January, never updated) become noise · 4 simultaneous progress bars compete for attention.

VAR 3.I

AI Insights Conversation Rail Tracker Insights AI · NOT the deferred DCP Coach

Conceptual model: the rail is a conversational extension of the existing Tracker Insights surface. Last insight bubble → suggested follow-up questions → input field. Members can ask about their data ("Why are my steps low?"), get AI-generated answers grounded in their tracker history, and see prior thread state. Not the deferred DCP AI Coach — that's a different surface (tied to specific care paths). This is the data-explanation AI we already have, given a permanent rail. Tests: does the AI Insights differentiator gain weight when conversational?

Rail contents

  • Most recent AI insight bubble at top (or thread of 2–3 turns if a recent conversation exists)
  • 3 suggested questions as chips (system-generated based on data state)
  • Input field for asking arbitrary questions
  • "↻ refresh" affordance regenerates the top insight
  • The in-content Tracker+Insights module no longer needs to render the insight separately — rail handles it. The module becomes tracker tiles only on desktop. (Mobile keeps the combined module since rail content folds.)

When this wins · risks

Wins when: the AI Insights system can reliably answer follow-up questions (current capability check needed) · members read the data and want to ask "why" · the differentiator vs. Personify's PercyIQ ("explain without acting") gets even stronger when it's conversational.

Risks: conversational AI raises expectations the system may not meet (cost: trust drop) · privacy / clinical-advice boundaries need clear rules · LLM costs scale with usage · members may use the rail for non-data questions ("what should I eat?") — needs scope-policing · easy to confuse with the deferred DCP AI Coach in stakeholder conversations.

VAR 3.J

Wellness Identity / Health Snapshot Rail

Conceptual model: the rail shows the member's clinical health-self at a glance. HRA score, key biometrics (BMI, BP, key labs), active conditions ribbon, risk indicators. Different from 3.A's identity card (which was avatar/name/lifestyle) — this is clinical identity. Connects to the biometrics + conditions feature areas. Tests: does giving health-self a permanent home increase HRA completion rates and DCP enrollment by keeping clinical context visible at every visit?

Rail contents

  • HRA score card — overall score with Good/Caution/At Risk badge · CTA to retake when stale
  • Biometrics card — 4 key metrics (BMI · Resting HR · BP · A1c) with per-row Good/Warn/At Risk badges
  • Active conditions card — disclosed conditions as tags + count of matched care paths
  • Empty/partial states: "Take HRA →" if no HRA done · "Sync biometrics →" if no biometrics · hide conditions card if none disclosed

When this wins · risks

Wins when: HRA completion is a primary KPI · clinical depth is the sales pitch (DCPs are interventions, not content) · the population includes members with disclosed conditions who'd benefit from clinical context · MHC's biometrics integration is a moat over Personify (lighter clinical features).

Risks: displaying clinical data on home raises privacy + UX concerns (who's looking over my shoulder?) · the "Elevated BP" badge surfaces health anxiety on every page load · disclosed conditions are sensitive — needs strict opt-in display rules · empty state for non-completers (no HRA, no biometrics) is a "do this work" prompt that feels naggy if seen daily.

VAR 3.K

Upcoming / Calendar Rail

Conceptual model: the rail is time-based — what's coming. Today's reminders, this week's events, deadlines, monthly milestones. Different from 3.B (which was "right now" / status) — this is "what's ahead." Aggregates time-bound signals from Programs (next session), Employer (open enrollment deadlines, fairs), Challenges (challenge end dates, raffle draws), and Tracker reminders (sleep wind-down). Tests: do members benefit from a calendar rail when life-event dates drive engagement?

Rail contents (4 sections)

  • Today — reminders for today (1–2 items max · sleep wind-down, scheduled session)
  • This week — events 0–7 days out (DCP sessions, fairs, scheduled meetings)
  • Deadlines — time-pressure items (open enrollment, challenge end dates, raffle draws) · highlighted with deadline color
  • This month (optional, hidden if empty) — milestones 8–30 days out

Aggregates from: Programs (next session times), Employer events, Challenges, AI Insight reminders, optional calendar feed integration.

When this wins · risks

Wins when: the population is deadline-motivated (open enrollment season is real) · employer events drive engagement (wellness fairs, biometric screening days) · challenge end dates create Goal-Gradient pressure · members are calendar-checkers by behavior.

Risks: requires aggregation logic across multiple sources — easy to miss items or duplicate · empty rail (no upcoming events) feels broken · time-bound items are stale fast — needs reliable cleanup · for clients without rich employer-event programs, half the rail is empty most of the time.

Recommendation across all 6 non-nav rails:
  1. 3.E (Engagement) remains the strongest demo-able variant — directly maps to the rewards configurability moat MHC sells.
  2. 3.B (Status) is the lowest-risk daily-return play; works for most populations.
  3. 3.J (Wellness Identity) is the most strategically interesting of the new four — leans into MHC's clinical-depth differentiator. But data dependency is high (members must complete HRA, sync biometrics, disclose conditions) and the "Elevated BP" daily-anxiety risk needs handling. Worth a UI pass to validate.
  4. 3.K (Calendar) answers the "open enrollment / employer event" use case real CS clients ask for. Good to validate with a UI pass.
  5. 3.H (Goals) requires a goals engine that doesn't fully exist today — block-fidelity only until the engine is in scope.
  6. 3.I (AI Conversation) is the riskiest — privacy/clinical-advice boundaries + LLM cost + scope creep into the deferred DCP AI Coach territory. Don't advance without a separate scoping conversation.

The home page can only have one rail. Pick the one that maps to the strongest engagement lever for the client population at hand. An employer-population client probably wants 3.K (Calendar — open enrollment). A reward-engagement client wants 3.E. A clinical-program client wants 3.J. This is per-client configurability territory — same building blocks, different rail content per client.
