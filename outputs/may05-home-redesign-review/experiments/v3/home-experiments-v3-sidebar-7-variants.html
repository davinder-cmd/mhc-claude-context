MHC Home — v3 · 7 Sidebar Variants calm + scannable

v3 · 2026-05-07 · Block fidelity · Desktop 1200 + Mobile 375

Seven variants exploring two structural changes from v2: (1) a sidebar on desktop (left, right, or both), and (2) a boxed hero that no longer extends full-width — it sits as a card within the content column, with the sidebar(s) consuming the rest of the horizontal space.

All seven carry forward the calm/scannable principle from v2.F: fewer items per section, generous whitespace, soft color treatment, single CTA per section. The v2 prominence ranking still applies (Hero w5 · Things I care about w4 · From your employer w4 · Programs DCP-only w3 · Tracker+Insights paired w3).

What's locked across all 7 variants:
  • Boxed hero — never full-bleed; always a card within the content column on desktop
  • v2 ranking — hero w5, lanes w4 (paired), DCP-only programs w3, Tracker+Insights paired w3
  • Calm rendering — 1–2 items per section, soft fills, generous gaps, single CTA per section
  • Mobile reflow — sidebar collapses to top hamburger or drawer; content goes single-column
  • 508 a11y, skeleton render for data-dependent sections, source labeling
What varies:
  • Sidebar position (left, right, both, none)
  • Sidebar content (nav only · nav + identity · nav + featured DCP · status rail · engagement rail · icon-only · dismissible)
  • Sidebar width (56px icon-rail · 180px standard · 240px wide)

Tests: does anchoring the page with a member-identity sidebar deepen the felt-sense of "this is my home" beyond what the greeting line alone can do?

Sidebar contents

  • Top: 6 nav links — Home (active), Programs, Trackers, Rewards, Library, Profile
  • Bottom: Member identity card — avatar, first name, week-summary stat ("52K steps this week"), interest list ("Sleep · Stress · Move more")
  • Width: 180px (standard left-nav width — tighter than the legacy MHC 220–240px)

When this wins · risks

Wins when: the page should feel like a personal workspace, not a publication · the member identity card carries trust-building work (you're known here) · the sidebar provides a reliable wayfinding column that doesn't compete with content for attention.

Risks: the identity card adds another personalization signal that can compete with the lanes (concern #4 from the meeting prep doc — "personalization signal is weak" — could become "too many personalization signals") · sidebar at 180px takes ~15% of horizontal real estate at 1200 width · doesn't address John's "eliminate side nav" goal directly.

VAR 3.B

Right Status Rail

Thesis: The top nav handles wayfinding; the right rail surfaces "Today" — next program step, tracker sync state, recent reward, quick continue action. The rail is glance-value, not navigation. Boxed hero in the main content column. Tests: does a small persistent right rail give returning members a 4-second status read without forcing them to scroll, while keeping the main content column calm?

Tests: is the sidebar's wayfinding value preserved when it's reduced to icons, while content reads at near-full-width?

Sidebar contents

  • 56px icon-only rail. Six icons — Home (active), Programs, Trackers, Rewards, Library, Profile (avatar at bottom)
  • Tooltip on hover/tap — labels appear so users learn the icons over time

When this wins · risks

Sidebar contents

  • Top: 6 nav links (same as 3.A)
  • Bottom: "Pick up where you left off" featured DCP card with name, session, progress bar, reward, Continue CTA
  • Featured DCP rules: most recently engaged active DCP wins the slot; ties broken by closest-to-completion

When this wins · risks

Risks: the featured DCP card is high-stakes — if logic picks the wrong DCP, it's wrong on every page load · pinning it to the sidebar means it always renders, even when the member just completed a session (consider state-aware hide/dim) · members without active DCPs see an empty rail bottom — needs an empty-state.

Tests: does the right rail give us a permanent home for the rewards moat without making it dominate the main content column?

Behavior

  • Default state: collapsed 56px icon rail (matches 3.C visual)
  • Toggle: button at bottom of rail. Click → expands to 200px with labels (matches 3.A visual minus the identity card)
  • Persistence: preference saved per device per member · sticky across sessions

When this wins · risks

VAR 3.G

Triple Column · Wide Desktop Only

Thesis: The most ambitious sidebar treatment. Wide desktop only (≥1280px or so): left nav (160px) + content (variable) + right status rail (180px). Below the wide breakpoint, gracefully collapses — first dropping the right rail, then the left nav. Tests: on truly wide displays, does giving wayfinding and status their own permanent columns let the content area focus exclusively on the lanes/programs/insights?

Breakpoint behavior

  • ≥ 1280px: full triple column · nav left + content + status right
  • 1024–1279px: drop right rail · nav left + content (matches 3.A footprint)
  • 768–1023px: drop left nav too · single column (matches mobile composition)
  • < 768px: hamburger drawer for nav · single column · status card promotes to top of content

When this wins · risks

Wins when: wide-desktop is a meaningful share of usage · admin/HR users on big monitors benefit from permanent wayfinding + status without scroll · most ambitious treatment if "use the full browser page" eng need is interpreted as "use it for permanent UI" rather than "stretch content."

Risks: highest eng cost of all 7 variants · 4 distinct breakpoints to design and test · members on narrower screens never see the right rail · a "wide-desktop-only feature" raises the question of whether it's worth building if a minority of users see it.
