Understanding Insomnia

5 min read Updated 3 days ago

Many people struggle to get adequate or restful sleep. Over 40 million people in the United States have chronic insomnia, and over 30% of the population will experience some form of insomnia in their lifetime. Insomnia is a sleep disorder characterized by trouble falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking up too early, and it results in a poor quality of sleep and daytime impairment.

Causes of Insomnia

The causes of insomnia can be a combination of these factors:

  • Psychological factors, such as stress, anxiety, and depression
  • Medical conditions, such as chronic pain, sleep apnea, restless leg syndrome, or other health issues
  • Lifestyle factors, such as nutrition, physical activity, or napping habits
  • Environmental factors, such as noise, light, temperature, or an uncomfortable sleep setting

When to see a doctor

If insomnia is making it hard to function during the day or you've been struggling for more than a few weeks, it's worth a conversation with your primary care provider. They can help rule out underlying conditions and recommend evidence-based programs — including cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I), which has stronger long-term results than sleep medications.
