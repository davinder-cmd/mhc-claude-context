Competitive Mood Board · Google Health
2026-05-29 · Prepared for Davinder Rehal
App Aesthetic Study · Launched May 19, 2026

A friendlier OS for wellness.

Google Health is what Fitbit became when it grew up inside Google. It launched ten days ago, replacing the Fitbit app with a four-tab Material You design — Today, Fitness, Sleep, Health — anchored by a Gemini-powered Health Coach. Where Fitbit was gamified and stat-heavy, Google Health is calmer, more conversational, and softer in language: "Stress score" is now "Resilience," and the AI tells you whether you're Optimal, Balanced, or Low. The brand promise: proactive, personal, adaptive — for the whole you.

Material You · expressive blobs, activity rings, floating chips
01 · Brand Essence

Google's voice. Fitbit's data. Gemini's coach.

This is the first time Google has put its full design language behind a consumer health app. The result reads as Material You with a clinical conscience — friendlier than Fitbit's old gamification, softer in tone than Withings, and powered by the same conversational AI Google ships everywhere else.

02 · Color

The Google rainbow, softened.

Google brought its full brand palette — blue, green, peach, red — but every color is dialed down. Pure Google blue is reserved for the logo and primary CTAs; the working palette is its softer cousins: lavender, mint, peach, and soft apricot. Material You tonalities dominate the actual app surface.

03 · Typography

Google Sans, all the way down.

Google Sans (Product Sans–derived) is the entire type stack. Bold at display sizes, regular for body, never italic. The personality lives in the gradient color treatment Google applies to single words — usually whatever the Gemini brand needs to claim ownership of.

04 · Voice & Messaging

Softer language for harder data.

The most strategic copy decision Google made: rename clinically-cold metrics with warmer human language. "Stress score" became "Resilience." "Menstrual health" became "Cycle health." Numeric scores became Optimal / Balanced / Low. The data is the same; the relationship with it is different.

"A new era for your wellness."— Launch tagline (Google blog, May 19 2026)
"For the whole you."— Hero positioning
"Optimal. Balanced. Low."— Resilience scale, replacing the old numeric Stress score
"Give me a 30-minute upper-body routine I can do at home."— Sample Coach prompt featured in launch materials
05 · Gemini Health Coach

The center of gravity is now a chat.

The Coach is not a feature in Google Health — it's the strategy. Gemini analyzes wearable data continuously and offers proactive recommendations throughout the day. The interface borrows from Gemini elsewhere: conic-gradient avatar, soft lavender chat surface, suggested-prompt chips.

Proactive, personal, adaptive.

The Coach surfaces unprompted insights based on your last 48 hours of data, then accepts natural language follow-ups. You can ask for a workout, a sleep plan, or a check-in on a metric. It remembers context within a session.

Visual cues: Gemini conic-gradient avatar, light lavender bubble, suggested-prompt chips on first open.

06 · Data Visualization

Rings return, softened.

Google Health revives the activity-ring pattern Fitbit largely abandoned, but draws them in Material You colors: red, green, blue concentric arcs instead of Apple's red/green/blue. Add to that customizable metric cards on the Today and Health tabs, and a horizontal Resilience meter that replaces the old Stress score's numeric badge.

08 · Signature Design Devices

Six moves that make this Google.

01 · Four Tabs

Today · Fitness · Sleep · Health

The same four-tab IA across all devices. Today and Health are user-customizable; Fitness and Sleep are fixed feature surfaces.

02 · Coach Avatar

Conic-gradient orb

The Gemini brand mark — a multi-color conic gradient circle — appears wherever the AI Coach surfaces. Single, consistent, recognizable.

03 · Gradient Word

One word in Gemini gradient

"Resilience," "Cycle health," "Coach." Whatever Gemini owns gets the blue→lavender→green text gradient.

04 · Soft Renames

"Stress" → "Resilience"

Clinical metric names softened deliberately. Numeric scores replaced by Optimal / Balanced / Low. Reading comprehension over precision.

05 · Material You Chips

Floating rounded suggestion chips

Above the Coach input. Pre-filled prompts ("Plan a workout," "Why am I tired?") in pill chips with subtle hairlines.

06 · Activity Rings, Reborn

Three concentric arcs, Google colors

Fitbit had moved away from rings; Google brought them back, now in red/green/blue Material You. Familiar pattern, new palette.

09 · So What

What this means for MHC.

Google Health is the platform threat we need to plan around, not just the brand we compare against. When the Pixel Watch + Google Health Coach become the default for a chunk of our member base, our differentiation can't be "tracking + insights" anymore — they'll already have that.

One steal, one to study, one strategic call.

  • Steal — soft language for clinical metrics. Google renaming Stress → Resilience, with words (Optimal/Balanced/Low) instead of a number, is the smartest copy decision of the year. Our DCP outcome metrics should follow — most of our members don't want a score, they want a verdict they can act on.
  • Study — the Gemini gradient word. A single word getting a chromatic treatment is more disciplined than full headline gradients. We could use it to mark our own A.I. surfaces consistently across DCP Expert, Insights, and Motivator Coach.
  • Strategic call — don't compete with the Coach on its turf. Google Health Coach will be the default conversational AI on Pixel + Fitbit. Our A.I. Health Insights is a daily summary tied to programs and outcomes — not a chat replacement. Our edge is the program, the clinical evidence base, and the cross-condition trust we earn over weeks. Lean into that. Don't try to out-chat Gemini.
  • Watch — Resilience as a metric. If "Resilience" becomes the language consumers use to talk about their stress data, we should align — or have a defensible reason not to. This is the kind of vocabulary shift that defines a category quickly.
