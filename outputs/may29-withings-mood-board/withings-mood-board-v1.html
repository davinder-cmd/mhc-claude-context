App Aesthetic Study

Numbers as protagonist.

Withings's Health Mate is the quietest health app in the category. There are no hero photos, no motivational illustrations on every screen, no aggressive color. The number is the story — measured, displayed huge, paired with a tiny sparkline and a one-word verdict. The brand sells European medical-grade hardware; the app behaves accordingly.

Editorial flat · runner on lavender hills · French magazine register
01 · Brand Essence

The register: precise, premium, polite.

Where Sword reads as humanitarian healthcare, Withings reads as European consumer electronics that happens to be clinical. The app feels like the Apple Watch's calmer, more medical sibling — restraint as luxury signal, data as proof of care.

Quantified Premium Restrained French-modern Editorial Clinically validated Lifestyle-medical Quiet Hardware-led Data-first
02 · Color

Cool neutrals. One indigo. Four pastels.

The page surface is a cool lavender-tinted gray — not warm cream like Sword. A single periwinkle indigo carries the brand. Four pastel category chips (Activity, Body, Heart, Cycle) are the only chromatic energy, and a coral red is reserved almost entirely for cycle tracking flow days.

03 · Typography

Numbers loud, copy quiet.

A neo-grotesque sans (close to SF Pro Display or a custom Withings variant) carries everything. The display move is not the headline — it's the metric. Numbers are set 60–96px and bold; labels above them are tiny and muted. The entire visual identity sits on this contrast.

04 · Voice & Messaging

Marketing speaks. The app barely does.

Two voices, intentionally separated. Web marketing leans into clinical credibility and ecosystem language. Inside the app, copy reduces to a status word ("Stable", "Normal", "High HR") under each number. No coaching, no motivational flourishes, no second-person warmth.

"360° picture of your health."— Marketing site
"Innovative measurements. Accurate information."— App Store
"Stable. Normal. High HR."— In-app status vocabulary (the entire copy register)
"Clinically validated. Developed by experts."— Recurring trust frame
05 · Data Visualization

The chart is the brand.

Three repeated visualization moves make Withings instantly recognizable: (1) the indigo sparkline with dotted nodes, (2) the muted vertical-bar daily-step plot, and (3) the cycle-tracking coral disc cluster. No rings. No gauges. No big stat-of-the-day card. Charts are small and live inside metric cards — they're evidence, not theater.

06 · Imagery & Illustration

No photography. Editorial illustration only.

This is the most distinctive choice Withings makes. Almost no member photography. Instead, the app uses occasional editorial illustrations — flat, slightly retro, with stylized figures and pastel landscapes — placed inside hero cards for moments like "A new week begins!" or "Quiet day." The visual register is closer to a French lifestyle magazine than a fitness app.

07 · Signature Design Devices

Six moves repeated on every screen.

01 · Metric Card

Icon → label → huge number → status → sparkline

This exact stack appears 8–12 times per dashboard. It is the atomic unit of the entire app.

02 · Pastel Chip Grid

Four category pills: Activity, Body, Heart, Cycle

The "Following" row uses peach / lilac / pink / apricot pastel cards as the primary IA at the top of Measure.

03 · One-Word Verdict

"Stable." "Normal." "High HR."

Every metric earns one status word. No paragraphs, no coaching, no narrative — the data carries the meaning.

04 · Indigo Sparkline

Tiny line + dot nodes, never full chart

The graph is small enough to live inside a card and confident enough to never need a Y-axis label.

05 · Missions

Three tasks. Green checks.

"Today's Missions" structure (e.g. "Step on your scale") gamifies engagement without leaderboards or streaks.

06 · Premium Gate

Withings+ as confetti card

The Health Improvement Score lives behind a paywall and is marketed with indigo confetti, the only "feature card" sparkle the brand permits.

08 · So What

What this means for MHC.

Withings and MHC have similar surface area — wearable data, daily measurements, programs — but radically different brand jobs. Withings sells $300 hardware to lifestyle-medical consumers; we sell behavior change to overburdened employees managing real conditions. The lessons are about restraint, not register.

Two moves to steal — one to study — one to reject.

  • Steal — number-as-hero. Withings's 96px metric value with a one-word status under it is the most economical health-data pattern in the category. Our wearable cards under-display the numbers. Make the value the protagonist; the chart is the supporting cast.
  • Steal — pastel category chips. Four small pastel-tinted cards (Activity, Body, Heart, Cycle) handle Withings's top-level IA without icons or color systems fighting each other. We could organize program areas similarly — a calm, single-row, color-coded nav that doesn't shout.
  • Study — illustration over photography. Withings can get away with editorial illustration because their hardware does the photography job on the marketing site. We can't. Our members need to see themselves represented; photography in the home and onboarding still earns its place for us.
  • Reject — the one-word voice. Withings ships "Stable. Normal." as its entire app copy register. Our users are managing 2–3 conditions, often with low health literacy and high anxiety. They need one more sentence of context than Withings gives. Restraint, yes — silence, no.
