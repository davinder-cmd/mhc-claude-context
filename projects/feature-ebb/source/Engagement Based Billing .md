5. ## Additional Comments (Michael)

     
* On pipeline I agree we should track EBB opps separately (or at least flag them) but the value should be what we believe the real EBB value is for those opps.  I can take our model and turn it into a simpler "calculator" to estimate ARR value.  At Sword we called this the "cARRculator".  
* On incentives, we should make sure we are clear the client is funding the incentives we're just leveraging claims billing to bill them.  I would say if the client wants to increase the $100 it should not come out of MHC margin; i.e. we should think of the $47.50 price as $37.50 revenue to MHC; $10 incentive funding from client.  
* We need to have a clause in the contract to make clear the incentive is funded by the employer, MHC is processing the incentive on behalf of the employer (like we do today) and we will not pay incentives if we have not collected cash to fund them.  
* We should plan for 60-90 day implementation w/ a new payer, but it could take a few extra weeks after initial launch to ensure claims are being processed correctly  
* On claims, we're finalizing pricing w/ Waystar but claims should be no more than 1% of revenue.  MHC will be responsible for all RCM activities, we will just use Waystar as the software to enable automation/scaling.  MHC will be submitting claims, following up on denials, rejections, reconciling cash collected, etc.  Waystar will provide clearinghouse submission, real-time eligibility checks and electronic remits.  
* Claims denied should be 0%.  The client (employer) is contractually obligated to pay 100% of charges.  There may be relatively uncommon situations where a member leaves the company after enrolling in a MHC program at which point they are no longer eligible.  In this case we'll need to set up a contractual clause and mechanism w/ the employer to invoice those directly.  
* Payment timing may take 30-60 days when first setting up a new client/payer but then should come down significantly once we're up and running (within 1-3 weeks after submitting claims).  We can submit claims as frequently as we want but need to balance that with operational efficiency as well as Waystar costs which increase based on number of claims.  
* On regulatory I don't see risk on our position that our offering is a wellness benefit.  That's how Hinge/Sword do it.  The only other thing is making sure we're clear on the incentive funding.  We will need to send a report to the employer (like we do today) so they can apply proper taxes/withholding on incentives paid.
