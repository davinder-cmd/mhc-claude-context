# Brand & Agency Studios — Reference

Studios whose work defines what "best of the best" looks like in product-and-brand fusion — brand expressed *through* a product, not just brand as a marketing layer bolted on top. Use this when the question is *what does a coherent product brand actually look like*, not just *is this visually consistent* (see `visual-design-experts.md`/`design-system-people-to-follow.md` for that) or *is this the right words* (see `content-design-experts.md`).

**Why agencies, not individuals:** every other file in `reference/` rosters people because the discipline is legible at the individual level (a heuristic, a typeface, a framework). Brand-as-product-voice is closer to a studio-level discipline — it's the fusion of strategy, identity, product, and writing operating as one team, which is why the unit of study here is the studio, not a single practitioner.

**Directly relevant to:** the brand gap identified in `strategy/` — MHC has voice guides and a product but no unifying *brand system* connecting them. These five studios are five different, deliberately non-redundant answers to how you'd close that gap.

## The Five — Different Angles, Not Five Versions of the Same Thing

### IDEO
**Known for:** Inventing "design thinking" as a named, teachable process; human-centered design methodology; Apple's first mouse, the shopping-cart redesign case study, decades of health/wellness client work

**The angle:** Process. IDEO's actual product is the *method* — empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test — more than any single deliverable. If the honest answer to "how do I even approach design thinking" is "I don't have a repeatable process," IDEO is where that process comes from. See `design-thinking-process.md`, which is built on their framework adapted for solo practice.

**Why it matters for MHC specifically:** Deep, longstanding health-sector client work (medical devices, health systems, wellness products) — not a generalist agency dabbling in health, a genuine domain history.

**Best content:** *Design Thinking* case studies (ideo.com/case-studies) · Tim Brown, *Change by Design* (book) · IDEO U courses

**What to learn:** Repeatable human-centered process, prototyping culture, how a method becomes a shared team language

---

### frog
**Known for:** Strategic brand identity fused with hardware and digital product design at enterprise scale; futures/foresight tooling; Hartmut Esslinger's original industrial-design lineage (early Apple, Sony)

**The angle:** Strategy-to-product fusion at scale. Where IDEO sells a process, frog sells a *point of view about where the category is going* and then builds the brand and the product to match it in one motion — brand isn't a separate workstream from the product roadmap.

**Why it matters for MHC specifically:** The clearest model for what "brand and product strategy as one discipline" looks like when the client isn't a startup — relevant as MHC's product matures past the founder-brand-instinct stage.

**Best content:** frogdesign.com case studies · frog's public writing on "designing the future," foresight methodology

**What to learn:** Foresight/futures thinking as a strategic input, brand-and-product-roadmap fusion, enterprise-scale identity systems

---

### Pentagram
**Known for:** The largest independent design consultancy structured as autonomous partners (Michael Bierut, Paula Scher, and ~20 others each running their own practice under one name); identity systems for MIT Media Lab, Citibank, the Public Theater, countless cultural institutions

**The angle:** Pure identity craft. The deliberate counterweight to IDEO and frog's process-first models — Pentagram's partners argue from typographic and visual rigor first, strategy second. If MHC's brand problem is genuinely visual (the mark, the type system, the identity itself feels under-designed), this is the corrective, not another round of process.

**Why it matters for MHC specifically:** The partner model itself is instructive — each partner has a distinct point of view rather than one house style, which is a useful reminder that "consistent brand" doesn't mean "one aesthetic forced onto every surface."

**Best content:** Michael Bierut, *How to Use Graphic Design to Sell Things* · Paula Scher's Public Theater work · *Abstract: The Art of Design* (Netflix), Scher and Bierut episodes

**What to learn:** Identity systems that scale without becoming sterile, typographic/visual rigor as a starting point rather than an afterthought, personality within a system

---

### Ammunition
**Known for:** Robert Brunner's studio; Beats by Dre, Polaroid Now, and — closest to MHC's actual taste anchor — the calm, restrained consumer-hardware aesthetic that Withings' design lineage sits within

**The angle:** Category-adjacent taste, made explicit. This is the direct answer to already liking Withings' design team: Ammunition (and Brunner's broader lineage, including his earlier work shaping Apple's industrial design language) is the pattern behind that instinct — premium restraint, clinical trustworthiness without feeling sterile, objects that read as *calm technology*. Naming the pattern makes it usable as a reference, not just a feeling.

**Why it matters for MHC specifically:** Health-adjacent consumer hardware is a narrow, specific design problem — trust and calm have to coexist with premium feel, and most agencies don't specialize in exactly that intersection. This one does.

**Best content:** Ammunition's public portfolio (ammunitiongroup.com) · Robert Brunner interviews on "calm technology" and restraint in consumer electronics

**What to learn:** Calm-technology restraint, premium-without-cold, the specific visual grammar of trustworthy consumer health hardware

---

### Koto
**Known for:** Dynamic, systemized brand identity for digital-native products — Duolingo's brand evolution, Uber Eats, The New York Times digital identity work

**The angle:** Brand as a living system expressed *in-product*, not a static guidelines PDF. Koto's work treats a logo, color system, and voice as components that flex across contexts (an app icon, a notification, a marketing page) rather than a fixed lockup applied uniformly — the most contemporary of the five, and the most directly applicable to a digital product where brand has to survive being expressed through interaction and motion, not just print collateral.

**Why it matters for MHC specifically:** Direct precedent overlap — Koto's Duolingo work is brand identity for exactly the engagement-loop, education-path product shape already referenced in `engagement-behavior-experts.md`. Worth studying the brand system alongside the mechanics.

**Best content:** koto.studio case studies, especially the Duolingo rebrand · their public writing on "brand systems" vs. "brand guidelines"

**What to learn:** Brand as a flexible system rather than a fixed asset kit, identity that holds together across app icon → notification → marketing surface, systemizing personality instead of just documenting it

## How These Five Differ, at a Glance

| Studio | Core Angle | Reach For When |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| **IDEO** | Process | You don't have a repeatable way to *approach* design thinking at all |
| **frog** | Strategy-to-product fusion | Brand and product roadmap feel like two separate workstreams |
| **Pentagram** | Identity craft | The problem is genuinely visual — the mark/type/system itself is under-designed |
| **Ammunition** | Category-adjacent taste | You need to name and reuse the "calm, trustworthy, premium health object" instinct you already have |
| **Koto** | Living brand systems | Brand needs to survive being expressed in-product, across icon/notification/marketing, not just on a guidelines PDF |

## How to Use This Reference

### When the brand problem is "I don't know where to start":
1. IDEO — borrow the process, see `design-thinking-process.md`
2. frog — for the strategic frame of where the brand needs to go, not just what it looks like today

### When the brand problem is visual/identity specifically:
1. Pentagram — typographic and identity rigor first
2. Ammunition — for the specific calm-clinical-premium register MHC should be aiming at

### When the brand needs to work *in-product*, not just as a guidelines document:
1. Koto — study the Duolingo case study directly, it's the closest precedent
2. Cross-reference `content-design-experts.md` for the voice half of the same problem

### When benchmarking against reference material from other org teams:
Per [[feedback_reference_not_driver]] — this file is for awareness and precedent, not for anchoring the direction. Use these five to sharpen a point of view, not to imitate one wholesale.
